Raising a grandchild or a relative's child

You stepped in when the children needed you. Whether you are their grandmother, aunt, uncle, big sister or a family friend, there is help for them and for you. Pick what you need first.

Becoming a licensed kinship foster parent

If children services placed the child with you, ask whether you can become a licensed foster parent.

Website

More about this: Becoming a licensed kinship foster parent

Children's Bureau, U.S. Administration for Children and Families

Since 2023, states may use licensing standards made for relatives, and licensed relative homes get the same foster care payments as other licensed foster homes.

Next step: Ask the caseworker what licensing would mean for your family, what the payments are, and how it compares with legal custody or guardianship. Free legal aid can help you decide.

Legal custody or a foster license? If you are caring for the children on your own, with no court order, you may need papers to enroll them in school or to take them to the doctor. A court can give you legal custody or guardianship, which lets you make those decisions. If children services placed the children with you, you can also ask about becoming a licensed foster parent, which comes with foster care payments. Each choice affects money, your rights and the parents' rights differently, so talk with free legal aid before you sign anything. Your state's GrandFacts sheet lists the options where you live.
If a parent is struggling with addiction. Many grandparents are raising grandchildren because of opioids or other drugs. The SAMHSA National Helpline is free and private, any hour, for the parent or for you. Taking care of yourself is part of taking care of them.
In Ohio? Raising a grandchild in Ohio covers OhioKAN, Ohio's kinship programs, the grandparent power of attorney and caretaker affidavit, cash help, Medicaid and school enrollment.

In danger right now? Call 911. To report a child in danger, call the Childhelp hotline at 800-422-4453.
