🇰🇷 South Korea: help for moms

Raising children in South Korea? If you or your child are in danger, call 112. South Korea's own services come first, such as 여성긴급전화 1366 (Women's Emergency Line), Danuri Call Center 1577-1366 (migrant women helpline) and 자살예방 상담전화 109 (suicide prevention line). Then come lines that work in any country. Pick what you need, or search. Today's kind word, near the end of the page, is in Korean, with the English under it. Choose another country.

여성긴급전화 1366 (Women's Emergency Line)

Emergency counselling, protection and shelter referrals, 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, for women facing domestic violence, sexual violence, prostitution, dating violence or stalking, and for their families and neighbours.

Call 1366Website

More about this: 여성긴급전화 1366 (Women's Emergency Line)

Ministry of Gender Equality and Family

Next step: Call 1366, with no area code. Migrant women are put through to the 이주여성1366 line for counselling in their own language.

Danuri Call Center 1577-1366

Phone counselling 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, in 13 languages, including emergency shelter and referrals for domestic violence, sexual violence and trafficking.

Call 1577-1366Website

Food and bills

Family payments, child benefits and one number that finds local help.1 option

부모급여 (parent allowance)

₩1,000,000 a month for a baby under 1 and ₩500,000 a month at age 1, in 2026.

Website

More about this: 부모급여 (parent allowance)

Ministry of Health and Welfare

If your child goes to daycare, the government pays the gap between the allowance and the daycare fee.

Next step: Apply at your local community service centre (you can do it when you register the birth), or online through Bokjiro or Gov24. Apply within 60 days of the birth and it is paid from the birth month. For questions, call the Ministry of Health and Welfare on 129.

Checked November 21, 2026. Sources: 1
