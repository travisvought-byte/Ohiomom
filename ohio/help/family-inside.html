Visiting someone in an Ohio prison

Every visitor, adult or child, must be on the person’s approved list before visiting. Each prison sets its own visiting hours.

Website

More about this: Visiting someone in an Ohio prison

Ohio Department of Rehabilitation and Correction

Adults send the visitor application with a copy of a driver’s license or state ID. A child needs the minor application and a copy of the birth certificate, and the birth certificate or custody papers at every visit. If the person inside is not on the child’s birth certificate and the child comes with someone other than a parent or guardian, a signed, notarized authorization form is also needed (it lasts one year).

Send the application to the prison itself by mail, fax or email, not to the central mail center. Once approved, register with ViaPath to book in-person or video visits. Registering before you are approved can slow it down.

Next step: Download the application from the visitation page, send it to the prison, and wait to hear back through your loved one.

What every Ohio jail must offer

Ohio’s jail standards set a floor for visits, mail and phone calls in county jails.

Website

More about this: What every Ohio jail must offer

Ohio Administrative Code 5120:1-8

A full-service jail must offer general visiting hours giving each person at least 30 minutes of visits a week. Video visits must be at least 20 minutes. Visiting hours must be posted.

The jail may not limit postal mail when the person pays the postage. If they have no money, the jail must give paper, envelopes and postage for two letters a week. They must have access to a phone and to their lawyer.

Every jail must have a grievance process with at least one appeal, and staff may not punish anyone for using it.

Next step: Ask the jail for its written visiting and mail rules, and keep a copy.

Helping your children

OhioKAN: help for relatives raising the children

A free state service for grandparents, aunts, older siblings and other kin raising a child, and for adoptive families.

Call 844-644-6526(844) OHIOKANWebsite

More about this: OhioKAN: help for relatives raising the children

Ohio Kinship and Adoption Navigator (OhioKAN)

An OhioKAN navigator talks with you one on one about what you need and what help is out there. Lines are open Monday to Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 6:30 p.m.

Next step: Call and ask for a navigator, or email one from the website.

If your loved one is a veteran

VA has outreach specialists for veterans in jail and court and re-entry specialists for veterans in prison, and the family can claim part of the VA pay held back during a prison stay.

Website

More about this: If your loved one is a veteran

Veteran Resource Guide (Veteran Home Guardians)

VA cuts disability pay during a prison stay after a felony conviction. A spouse, children or dependent parents can ask for part of what VA holds back, but VA does not send it unless the family applies.

Next step: Read the guide’s page, then apply on VA Form 21-0788.
