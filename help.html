Safety, crisis support and urgent needs. All free, most open any hour.

Call, text or chat. You do not have to give your name.

🧺 Food, bills and housing

Groceries, WIC, energy bills and the one number that finds local help.

SNAP: help buying groceries

U.S. Department of Agriculture, Food and Nutrition Service

SNAP helps households with low income buy food. Each state runs its own program, so the website, phone number and rules depend on where you live.

Open the state directory and choose your state. It links to your state's SNAP page and how to apply.

Website

Checked November 15, 2026. Sources: 1

WIC: food for pregnancy, babies and kids under 5

U.S. Department of Agriculture, Food and Nutrition Service

WIC gives healthy foods, nutrition advice, breastfeeding support and referrals at no cost. It is for women who are pregnant, recently pregnant or breastfeeding, and for babies and children under 5.

Choose your state on the apply page, then call or apply online with a WIC agency near you. Bring ID for each person, proof of address, and proof you get SNAP, Medicaid or TANF, or proof of income.

Website

Checked November 15, 2026. Sources: 1 2

Help with heating and cooling bills (LIHEAP)

Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services

LIHEAP can help pay a share of your energy bills for households with low income. Rules and how to apply differ by state. Applying never costs a fee.

Call the National Energy Assistance Referral line, weekdays 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. Eastern, or visit EnergyHelp.us. Give your city, county and state.

🍼 Pregnancy and new baby

Health coverage, nursing support, diapers and a safe place for baby to sleep.

Low-cost prenatal care at a community health center

HRSA health centers, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services

Health centers funded by HRSA charge on a sliding fee scale, so you can get care even if you are uninsured or cannot pay. Free or reduced-cost prenatal care may also be available through your area health department.

Search by ZIP code on the HRSA site, call the center, and ask whether it provides prenatal care and what the sliding fee would be. Or call 1-800-311-BABY to find your area health department.

1-800-311-BABY

Health coverage for your child: Medicaid and CHIP

Insure Kids Now, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services

Medicaid and the Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP) cover many children in families with low or moderate income. Each state sets its own rules.

Open the coverage page, choose your state, and follow the steps to apply. Questions can go to the national line.

Breastfeeding questions: free helpline

Office on Women's Health, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services

Breastfeeding peer counselors answer questions, help with common problems and point you to other help, in English or Spanish. It is an information line and does not give medical advice.

Call Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern; it is closed on federal holidays. WIC also offers breastfeeding support.

La Leche League

Meetings and one-to-one help led by trained volunteer Leaders, who have breastfed their own babies. A map shows groups and Leaders near you and online.

Free diapers from a diaper bank

National Diaper Bank Network member banks

Diaper banks collect and give out free diapers to families who struggle to afford them. The national directory lists member banks by state and city.

Open the directory, find your state, and contact the bank nearest you to ask how to sign up. Rules differ by bank. If none is close, dial 211 and ask about diaper help.

Safe sleep for your baby

Safe to Sleep campaign, National Institutes of Health

Put your baby to sleep on their back, on a firm, flat surface made for babies, with nothing soft in the sleep space. This lowers the risk of sleep-related death.

Read the short guides, in English and Spanish, before the first night home.

🧩 My child learns or grows differently

Autism, ADHD, delays and disabilities: free evaluations, school help, other parents and respite.

CDC milestone checklists and Milestone Tracker app

Centers for Disease Control and Prevention

Free checklists show what most children do at each age, from 2 months to 5 years. They come in English and Spanish, and there is a free phone app.

Fill out the checklist for your child's age. Take it to your child's next doctor visit. You do not need a diagnosis to ask for help.

CDC-INFO

Find your state's early intervention program

Centers for Disease Control and Prevention

Every state has free early intervention for children under 3 who are behind or have a disability. You can call yourself. You do not need a doctor's note.

Look up your state's number and ask for a free evaluation. If your child is 3 or older, call your public school district instead.

Find your state on the map. Call before your next school meeting.

SSI for children with disabilities

Social Security Administration

Monthly payments for children with serious disabilities in families with low income and savings. In most states, SSI also means Medicaid. At 18, Social Security checks again using adult rules.

Start online or call for an appointment. Have medical and school records ready.

National Alliance on Mental Illness

NAMI Basics is a free 6-week class for parents and caregivers of children and teens with mental health symptoms, taught by parents who have been there. It is offered in person or online. The HelpLine gives information and support. It is not a crisis line.

Find a class through your local NAMI or take the online version. Call the HelpLine on weekdays.

One page per state gathers links for finding licensed child care, help paying for it, child support, health coverage and more.

Head Start and Early Head Start: free early learning

Office of Head Start, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services

Free learning and development for children from birth to age 5 in eligible families. Early Head Start serves babies and toddlers, and some programs also serve pregnant women. All programs welcome children with disabilities.

Use the center locator, then call the program listed and ask how to enroll. If there is no space, ask to be added to the waiting list.

Free, private support for anyone who is pregnant or has had a baby and feels sad, anxious or overwhelmed. Counselors answer any hour, in English and Spanish.

🎖️ Military and veteran families

Help built for service members, veterans and their families.

Military OneSource special needs consultations

U.S. Department of Defense

Free consultants help military families with special education, TRICARE, EFMP and local services. Retirees and honorably discharged members can use it for one year after leaving service.

Call any time or start a chat to set up a phone or video meeting.

Call 800-342-9647Website

Checked November 8, 2026. Sources: 1

Exceptional Family Member Program (EFMP)

Each military service branch

Active duty families sign up a family member with special medical or school needs. This helps the military plan moves and support around those needs. Some TRICARE programs require it.

Contact the EFMP office at your base, or call Military OneSource.

Call 800-342-9647Website

Checked November 8, 2026. Sources: 1

CHAMPVA health coverage for veterans' children

U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs

Health coverage for children of veterans rated permanently and totally disabled by a service-connected condition (or who died from one), if the family cannot get TRICARE. It usually ends at 18, or 23 for full-time students. A child who could not support themselves before 18 may stay covered.

Check if you qualify and apply on VA.gov, or ask your county veterans service officer to help.
