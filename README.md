# Yoga Mom Talk and OhioMom

One site for mothers and caregivers who need real help and a place to connect.

- **Yoga Mom Talk** (https://www.yogamomtalk.com/) is the national home: source-linked resources for all 50 states and DC. It is Deborah Vought's project and the primary site.
- **OhioMom** (https://www.ohiomom.org/) is the Ohio home of the same site and lives under `/ohio/`. It is one way in, with Ohio county pages, the Ohio family guide, listening and reporting forms.

Yoga Mom Talk is independent and is not affiliated with any other "Mom Talk" show or site.

## How it works

National site (Yoga Mom Talk):

- `data/ymt/resources.json` holds the site block and the 44 national resources in nine sections. `data/ymt/state-templates.json` and `data/ymt/states.json` expand into one record per state for 51 places (816 state records). Each listing has a plain-language description, a next step, a phone or website, the published source it was checked against, and the date it was checked.
- `python3 scripts/ymt_validate.py` checks the data. `python3 scripts/ymt_build.py` builds every national page, `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml`; `--check` confirms what is on disk matches the data.
- `python3 -m unittest discover tests` runs safety and integrity tests: the exit button and referrer policy on every national page, no third-party loads or tracking code, internal links and anchors resolve, phone links match the printed numbers, state pages use their own state, old Ohio URLs still resolve, and the Ohio pages and family guide are kept.
- The site name and domain live in one place, the `site` block of `data/ymt/resources.json`.

Ohio site (OhioMom):

- `data/resources.json` holds the statewide Ohio listings, housing and shelter included; `data/family-guide.json` owns the complete Ohio family guide.
- `python3 scripts/build.py` builds `family-guide.html` from `data/family-guide.json`, `data/family-counties.json` and `templates/family-guide.html`. The pages under `/ohio/` are static source: a statewide listing is kept in `data/resources.json` and written into the page that shows it, and a county page is the only home of its local listings.
- `ohio/all.html` ("All resources") is not built ahead of time. Its script, `ohio/assets/all.js`, reads the other Ohio pages, the Ohio page of the national site, the family guide and the moms groups in the visitor's browser and lays every listing out once, so it cannot fall out of step with them. A new county page appears in it as soon as `ohio/assets/data.json` names the page.
- `ohio/assets/places.json` lets that page turn a town or ZIP code into a county. `python3 scripts/ohio_places.py US.txt state_county_zip.json DATE` rebuilds it from two source files that are not kept here; the top of the script says where they come from and how the two are reconciled.
- `python3 scripts/validate.py` checks the Ohio data. `npm test` checks the family guide, the page layouts, the Ohio housing page and the All resources page.

## County pages, for every county

Every county in the 50 states and D.C. has a page with local listings from six federal directories, and every state page has a picker that leads to them. Six Ohio counties also have a hand-checked guide under `ohio/counties/`, and their county page links to it.

- **What a county page shows.** Seven closed rows, housing first: where to apply for public housing and Housing Choice Vouchers (Section 8), HUD-approved housing counselors, free legal help, low-cost health care (health center sites), school districts to ask for a free evaluation for a child from age 3, Head Start and Early Head Start, and a row of links to the same needs on the state's own page.
- **Free legal help.** The legal aid program funded by the Legal Services Corporation whose service area holds the county, with its intake phone number and website: help with custody, child support, protection from abuse and eviction for people with low incomes, as LSC describes legal aid. Counties are placed with LSC's own service-area map, which is a little simplified, so a county whose middle point falls offshore (San Francisco) is placed by its ZIP codes. Every county has one.
- **Public housing agencies.** A county lists every agency with its office there or named for it (Morrow Metropolitan Housing Authority keeps its office in Knox County, so it is Morrow's agency and only a nearby one for Knox), largest first, then the nearest others in the state up to three, then the state's own voucher program where the state runs one (a code like `MI901` with at least 1,000 vouchers). HUD's names are cut off at 45 characters and carry a few typos ("Metropolitian"); `agency_name` puts back a cut word only where the city or a short list of words makes it certain. Voucher programs that a service provider runs for one group of people (codes like `MN801`) are left out. HUD's list has no websites, so these cards have a phone number only.
- **School districts.** Regular public districts (NCES types 1 to 3) that are open, run at least one school, and teach kindergarten or younger. A district is listed in every county its boundary covers, from NCES's school district relationship file, the districts covering the most of the county first (a county holding less than one square mile of a district counts only if it holds the most of it). Placing districts by where their office or schools are does not work: several Alaska districts are run from Anchorage, and some Texas districts have virtual or partner campuses near Houston. For the few districts with no boundary on file, the counties of their schools are used, leaving out a school far from the others. Three Virginia cities have no district of their own and show the nearest. Districts named only "X County" or "X City" are shown as "X County Schools", as they are called locally. The row starts with three lines on asking for an evaluation, from the Center for Parent Information and Resources and the federal rule on written notice (34 CFR 300.503), and sends a parent of a child under 3 to the state's early intervention card. When a county has no health center or Head Start site of its own, the row shows the nearest three within 60 miles and says how far each is from the middle of the county; housing counselors are those in the county, then the nearest within 75 miles. A long list shows six cards, "Show more", and a box to narrow it by town or name. Cards have the same parts as every other card on the site, and the three crisis phone cards stay in the open below the rows.
- **Where the listings come from.** Health centers: HRSA's Health Center Service Delivery and Look-Alike Sites file. Head Start: the Office of Head Start's daily list of service locations. Housing counselors: HUD's housing counselor list, state by state. Public housing: HUD's Public Housing Authorities open data file. Legal aid: LSC's list of the programs it funds (from its legal aid finder) and its service-area map (the file name carries a date; `SOURCES` says where to find the next one). School districts: the NCES Common Core of Data district directory for 2025-26, the NCES EDGE district and school location files for 2024-25, and the NCES school district relationship files for 2025 (which counties each district's boundary covers; reading it needs `openpyxl`). Counties, populations and county centers: the Census Bureau's 2024 county estimates, 2023 Gazetteer files and 2020 ZIP code (ZCTA) to county file. `python3 scripts/county_data.py --fetch SRC` downloads all of them (about 90 MB, kept out of the repo); `SOURCES` at the top of the script has every address, and says where to look for next year's NCES files.
- **What is left out, and why.** School-based, mobile-van, nursing-home, prison and administration-only health center sites (not places a mom can walk in); closed Head Start locations; any listing without a working-looking phone number (a placeholder like 000-000-0000 or a 555 number); and any site, program or agency whose name or street says it is a shelter, a crisis or respite unit, a residential program, or a confidential location (`SENSITIVE` and `CARE_HOME` in the script, with a test). Domestic violence locations are never published, and a clinic inside a shelter serves the people staying there. HUD agencies with status `C` are left out; HUD does not publish what its status codes mean, and every `P` agency in the list names a parent agency.
- **Website buttons only for websites that loaded.** `python3 scripts/county_links.py SRC` requests every website address the directories give (about 14,600, most of them school districts) and keeps the answers in `data/county-links.json`. A card gets a Website button only when its site loaded, or turned the robot away while clearly being there; broken, parked, forwarded and unreachable sites keep their phone number and lose the button. On November 16, 2026, 13,154 of 14,617 addresses passed. `python3 scripts/county_links.py --shards` checks the linked ones again.
- **Rebuilding.** `python3 scripts/county_data.py SRC` writes `data/ymt/counties.json` and `assets/us/`, then `python3 scripts/ymt_build.py` rebuilds the pages (the state pickers come from `counties.json`). The listings carry the download date. Building needs `numpy` and `openpyxl`; the tests do not.
- **Kept current every month.** On the 5th, `.github/workflows/county-refresh.yml` downloads all six directories again, checks every website address again, rebuilds, runs every check, and opens a pull request with the coverage report (or, if the repository does not let workflows open pull requests, an issue naming the branch). A pull request opened by a workflow does not start the usual checks by itself, which is why the job runs them first. It can also be run by hand from the Actions tab. Merging it does not publish.
- **Connecticut** is listed by its nine planning regions, the county-equivalents the Census Bureau and HRSA have used since 2022. Head Start and the ZIP code file still use the old eight counties there, so those sites and ZIP codes are placed in a region by the nearest town.
- **Privacy.** The page reads only this site's own files, with no cookies sent. The county is in the part of the address after `#`, which a browser never sends to the server. A typed ZIP code is matched inside the browser; the only request it causes is for the list of ZIP codes that start with the same digit.
- **Why one page and not 3,144.** Wix hosts at most 2,000 files and 20 MB for a site, so the county layer is one page plus 61 data files (about 7 MB, which Wix compresses about five to one on the way to a phone). The whole site is about 12 MB. It is too big to publish through the Wix connector; it goes live with `scripts/publish_wix.py` (see "Publishing to Wix").
- **How much is done.** `python3 scripts/coverage.py` reports it. On November 16, 2026: every county has a page, and every county has at least one listing inside the county itself; 3,141 (99.9%) have a school district in the county; 2,033 (64.7%, home to 89.6% of Americans) have a public housing agency with its office there or named for it; 2,968 (94.4%, 99.3% of people) have all six kinds of listing in or near the county; every county has a legal aid program serving it; and 6 counties (0.2%, 1.1% of people) have a hand-checked local guide.

## How pages are laid out: a little first, the rest when asked

A mom arrives with one need. The pages show her the choice, then a short list, then the details, in that order.

- **Front pages show no listings.** `index.html` and `ohio/index.html` ask what she needs, offer the needs to choose from and a way to pick her state or area, and stop. Each has "Housing and shelter" as a choice of its own and ends with "See everything". The national front page also keeps three small phone cards for emergencies. Each front page has a small picture beside the question, with the joke of the day in a speech bubble in it (see "The picture and the joke of the day").
- **Housing and shelter is a need of its own.** It leads with a shelter search, the women's shelter list for the state and the domestic violence hotline, then rent help, public housing, housing counselors and the organizations that shelter families. Shelter addresses are never printed.
- **Everything is on one page, and it can be searched.** `help.html` holds every national listing. A state page holds everything for that state: the state's own offices first, then every national listing the state has no version of (`REPLACED_BY_STATE` in `scripts/ymt_build.py` says which those are). Both pages have a search box and a "Show everything" button (`assets/ymt.js`). Searching hides what does not match and opens the needs that do; words match where a word starts, and a short list of everyday words ("homeless", "daycare", "food stamps") reaches the listing that uses another word. `help.html#everything` and `help.html#q=rent` do the same from a link. The box only filters what is already on the page: it has no name and no form, so nothing typed can be sent, and it stays hidden when the script does not run.
- **OhioMom has the same two doors.** `ohio/help/housing.html` has three rows: a place to stay tonight, leaving an unsafe home, and rent, eviction and finding a home. Each row opens five or more listings, Ohio's own first. Two phone cards (211 and the domestic violence hotline) stay in the open above the rows, with the quick way to leave the page beside them. `ohio/all.html` is every OhioMom listing in one list, grouped by need. It can be searched, narrowed to one need, and narrowed to a county by picking the county or by typing a town or ZIP code. A local office carries its county's name and comes first once that county is chosen; a county with no page yet says so and shows what works statewide. A ZIP code that crosses a county line names the other county and offers it. The county she picks is saved the same way the area picker saves it (in her browser only); what she types is never sent or saved, and only the need is ever written into the page address.
- **Needs are closed rows.** On `help.html` and every state page each need is a `<details class="need">` that stays closed until she opens it. A link that names a need (`help.html#food-bills`) or a listing inside one opens it for her (`assets/ymt.js`).
- **Cards are short.** A card shows the name, one line (the first sentence of `what`), and the Call and Website buttons. The organization, the rest of the description, the next step, the sources and the checked date sit behind "More about this".
- **The inner OhioMom pages work the same way.** County pages, `ohio/help/housing.html`, `ohio/help/pregnancy-baby.html` and `ohio/wellbeing.html` are an index of closed rows; `ohio/help/bills.html` and `ohio/help/single-parent.html` are a handful of short cards. Each ends with a link to every listing for that need on the All resources page, and each county page links to everything for that county. Searching a page opens the rows that match and puts the others away. `ohio/connect.html` and the area panel on `ohio/near-you.html` are rows by kind of group. `ohio/events.html` shows the next six events and a "Show all" button, and each event keeps its times, place, price and source behind "When, where and cost" (`ohio/assets/site.js`).
- **Crisis numbers are never behind a tap.** `ohio/wellbeing.html` and `ohio/help/housing.html` keep small phone cards in the open above their rows, the way the national front page does. Each one repeats a listing further down the same page. The "Leave this page quickly" link sits with them, in the open, because a domestic violence line is showing; on county pages it is inside the "Safety at home" row, which shows nothing until it is opened.
- **The family guide asks before it lists.** `family-guide.html` opens with one question (how old is your child?) and six stages. Nothing is listed until a choice is made: a stage, a county, a need, or a link that names one listing. A choice shows the first calls for that stage and the first six programs as short cards, with a "Show all" button. Glossary, changes, rights, military programs, good-to-know notes and the shareable flyer are closed rows. It wears the OhioMom look (`ohio/assets/site.css`) with its own small stylesheet. Without JavaScript the whole list shows. `tests/family-guide.cjs` holds all of this.
- A printed page opens everything.
- `tests/ymt-pages.cjs` and `tests/test_ymt.py` fail the build if a listing appears on a front page, a need is open by default, or the short part of a card grows. `tests/ohio-directory.cjs` fails it if a listing on any Ohio page is missing from All resources or appears there twice, if a town or ZIP code lands in the wrong county, or if the page asks for anything but this site's own pages.

## The picture and the joke of the day

Both front pages carry two things that are not help listings, added November 17, 2026 at the owner's request and modeled on the-reefer.com. `scripts/home_extras.py` makes both, for both pages, so they cannot drift apart.

- **The picture.** Beside the question (above it on a phone) is a small moving picture: a mom holds tree pose on her mat and tells the joke of the day, and her child laughs and asks for it "Again! AGAIN!" until she opens one eye, and then says "Please?" OhioMom's is the same room with a cardinal at the window and Ohio on the wall. The drawing is inline SVG. The rules that move it are in `templates/home-extras.css`, and the script copies them to the end of `assets/ymt.css` and `ohio/assets/site.css` between marker comments: edit the template, then run `python3 scripts/home_extras.py`. The drawing is decoration, so it is hidden from screen readers, nothing in it takes the keyboard, it is left out of a printed page, and on a device set to reduce motion it holds still.
- **The joke of the day is in the picture.** It is the mom's speech bubble, at the top of the picture card, so it is the first thing a visitor meets. The owner asked for that on November 17, 2026: the joke should be told by the people in the picture and should disarm a visitor as she arrives (it first sat at the bottom of the page, where nobody found it). The bubble is HTML, not part of the drawing, so the words wrap on a phone and a screen reader reads them. The cost is known and accepted: on a phone the picture and joke together push the list of choices down by about a third of a screen.
- **Which joke shows.** `data/jokes.json` holds one joke for each date, each marked `approved` or not. The owner approves each batch of about 30 before it runs. An unapproved joke is never written into any page, and until a batch is approved the picture has no bubble and there is no `jokes.html`. Once the owner has approved a batch: `python3 scripts/home_extras.py --approve` (add `--start 2026-12-17` to move the batch to start on another day), then `python3 scripts/ymt_build.py` and `python3 scripts/home_extras.py`, and publish. The bubble starts hidden. A few lines of script in the page show the joke for the visitor's own date, so it changes at midnight on her clock; nothing is sent or stored. When a batch runs out the list starts over, one a day, so the bubble never goes missing, but draft the next batch for approval before then. `jokes.html` lists the ones that have run, newest first, and does not give away tomorrow's. Both sites show the same joke.
- **What keeps it in step.** `python3 scripts/home_extras.py --check` runs on every push and fails if the OhioMom front page or either stylesheet does not match what the script makes, or if the joke file has a gap in its dates, a repeat, or markup. `tests/test_home_extras.py` and `tests/home-extras.cjs` hold the rest: only approved jokes reach a page, the joke follows the date and sits in the picture, and the drawing stays decoration.

## Built so everyone can use it

- **One banner, one main, nothing loose.** Every page has a single page header and a single `<main>`, and everything a visitor meets sits inside one of them, including the "Leave this site" button and the "Did you find what you came for?" question.
- **Headings never skip a level.** Where a list of cards or events has no visible heading of its own, it has one for screen readers (`class="sr"`).
- **Row headings are valid HTML.** The `<h2>` of a closed row is a direct child of its `<summary>`; a CSS grid lays out the icon, the heading, the one-line summary and the count.
- **Text on buttons passes contrast.** White text on the pink (`--brand`) and teal buttons is at least 4.5:1.
- **Keyboard and touch.** Rows, cards and buttons are native elements with a visible focus ring. "More about this" has a tap area a thumb can hit.
- `tests/ymt-pages.cjs` and `tests/test_ymt.py` hold all of this. Every kind of page, the family guide included, was also run through axe (WCAG 2.2 A and AA rules) at phone and desktop widths on 2026-11-15 with no violations. The two front pages and the jokes list were run again on 2026-11-17, with the picture and a joke showing, with none.

## When someone shares a link

- **A picture goes with it.** Every page names a 1200 by 630 picture (`og:image`) that a phone, Facebook or a group chat shows beside a shared link: `assets/share.jpg` for Yoga Mom Talk, `ohio/assets/share.jpg` for OhioMom and `assets/share-family-guide.jpg` for the family guide. The address is always the full `https://www.yogamomtalk.com/...` one, because those apps do not follow relative addresses.
- **The tab and the home screen show our icon.** `assets/icon.svg` is the drawing; `assets/icon-48.png` and `assets/icon-180.png` are the same drawing for browsers and iPhones that want a fixed size.
- **The pictures are made, not edited.** `scripts/share_cards.py` fills in `templates/share-card.html` and photographs it. It is not part of the build; run it only to change a card, look at the result, and commit the pictures. Words on a card are a promise in a picture, so they must stay true of the site ("Free. No sign-up, no ads."). `tests/test_ymt.py` checks that every page names a picture that exists and has the right size.
- An app that already showed an old preview keeps it for a while. Facebook's Sharing Debugger and a new message thread on a phone both fetch the current one.

## Keeping it current

- Every push runs both validations, the build checks, the Python tests and the page tests.
- The jokes of the day run in dated batches. The owner approved the first on November 17, 2026, and it runs from that day to December 16, 2026. Before the last date in `data/jokes.json`, draft the next batch of about 30, get the owner's approval, and add them (see "The picture and the joke of the day").
- The Ohio events and moms groups live in `ohio/assets/data.json`, and the page script draws them in the browser. `scripts/prerender_ohio.py` also writes them into `ohio/events.html` and `ohio/connect.html`, so search engines, link previews and anyone with scripts turned off see the full lists. After changing `data.json`, run `python3 scripts/prerender_ohio.py`; the push checks fail until the pages match. The written lists carry the date they were written and include the events still on as of that day, so rerun it now and then to drop past events from the written copy. Visitors with scripts never see a past event: the script redraws the lists for their own day and area. The written markup copies the card and event templates in `ohio/assets/site.js`, and `tests/ohio-prerender.cjs` fails if the two drift apart.
- On the 3rd of each month every outside link on the Ohio pages is requested automatically (the statewide listings, every link written into a county page, the family guide, and the moms groups), and on the 4th the national ones (`scripts/linkcheck.py` does the work for both). Either check can also be run by hand from the Actions tab; a run on a branch other than `main` is a trial and opens no issue.
- A robot cannot settle every link, so each one is sorted: it loaded; it is **broken** (gone, failing, parked, taken over, or a page that says "not found"); it has **moved** (the address now forwards to a different website); or it **cannot be checked** (the site refuses robots). The last three open an issue, or add to the one already open, because a person has to look.
- `data/link-checks.json` records each link a person opened in a real browser and found working, with the date. The checks skip those for 180 days and then ask again. Add or re-date an entry only after opening the link yourself.
- A page that a search engine or a fetching tool still shows is not proof it is there. The first run of the wider Ohio check, on November 15, 2026, found six county and family-guide links that had died, one of them the family guide's link for finding a county board of developmental disabilities. Each was opened in a real browser before it was replaced.
- A link that loads is not proof the listing is right. On November 15, 2026 two parent-center addresses loaded fine for the robot while one forwarded to a gambling site and the other was parked for sale; one phone number was simply wrong. Those were found by comparing every state record with the national lists it is sourced from: the NDRN member list (disability-rights agencies), the CDC early intervention contact list, and the Parent Center Hub. Repeat that comparison a couple of times a year.

## Safety standards

- A fixed "Leave this site" button is on every national page. It jumps to an everyday page and does not erase browser history; the About page says so.
- The national pages carry no cookies, tracking, sign-up forms or third-party scripts of our own, and the report page uses a mail link only. Wix adds its own script and cookies to every page it serves, so the pages say that plainly and do not claim to be cookie-free.
- Domestic violence shelter addresses are never listed. Listings point to the hotline or program that places people safely.

## Publishing to Wix

Merging a pull request does not change the live site by itself. There are two ways to publish.

**By hand, through the Wix connector.** The changed files are sent through the connector, each one checked by fingerprint against the live copy before and after. This only works for changes small enough to pass through a conversation, so it cannot carry the county data in `assets/us/` (about 5 MB).

**From a shell, signed in with the Wix command line (how the county pages went live, November 16, 2026).** `npx @wix/cli@latest login` prints a link and a code; the owner of the Wix account opens the link, enters the code and approves. The command line needs Node 24 or newer. Then `WIX_TOKEN=$(npx @wix/cli@latest token) WIX_SITE_ID=84aafa61-13f9-4aac-b6f9-65bb88312105 python3 scripts/publish_wix.py --check`, and `--publish` to send. The token is short-lived and is never printed, and `npx @wix/cli@latest logout` ends the sign-in.

**From GitHub (built, waiting for a key).** `.github/workflows/publish.yml` runs `scripts/publish_wix.py`:

- **check** compares the live site with the repository and changes nothing.
- **publish** sends the files, then reads the site back and compares again. It refuses to run if what is live is not the last upload, it never deletes a live file, and it only publishes from `main`.

To switch it on:

1. In Wix, generate an API key ([Wix's steps](https://dev.wix.com/docs/api-reference/articles/authentication/api-keys/generate-an-api-key)). Limit its site access to this site. Wix shows the key once, so keep the window open for step 2. Wix's steps also say where to find the account ID.
2. In this repository on GitHub: Settings, Secrets and variables, Actions, New repository secret. Add `WIX_API_KEY` (the key) and `WIX_ACCOUNT_ID` (the account ID). `WIX_SITE_ID` is optional; without it the site is found by its name, OhioMom.
3. Actions, "Publish to Wix", Run workflow, mode **check**. It should report that the live site matches the repository.

What is not yet proven: the calls are the ones in Wix's recipe for uploading a site, and Wix says API keys are meant for automation such as GitHub Actions, but Wix's recipe shows these calls with a login token and does not say outright that they accept an API key. The first check run settles it and changes nothing either way. If Wix refuses the key, the run says so, and publishing stays by hand. Once a check and one publish have gone through, adding `push: branches: [main]` to the workflow makes every merge publish itself.

`tests/test_publish.py` runs the script against a stand-in for Wix: the complete file set is sent, nothing live is deleted, a refused key or an unexpected live state stops everything, and the key is never printed.
